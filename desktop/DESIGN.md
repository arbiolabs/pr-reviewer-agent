# pr-review desktop: the design

The app answers one question: what needs me right now? The home is a queue of decisions, most urgent first, each one a card with its key fact, one primary action and its shortcut. Around it sit a calm board (a live strip of running reviews, every PR in the sidebar) and a detail pane that reads like a document: a serif title, one sentence on where things stand, then the argument and the code it rests on.

Three directions were built and judged against the product brief. The triage queue won as the base. From the editorial direction it takes the typographic voice, the state sentences, the quoted finding and the phase-sectioned timeline. From the workstation direction it takes the two-column finding, the scrubbable live track and the palette that explains itself.

## The queue

`src/queue.rs` reads the TUI's `Model` as cards. It holds no state, so the queue can't drift from the reducer.

| Rank | Card | Headline | Primary action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Setup | Reviews can't run: no models available | Open Settings |
| 1 | Approve | the PR's title, "Posts 2 findings · 1 high, 1 medium" | Review post, then ⌘⏎ posts after one sheet |
| 2 | Posting | the PR's title, what GitHub said | Open |
| 3–4 | Findings (critical, high) | the PR's title, the top finding quoted in the serif | Open finding |
| 5 | Failed | the PR's title, the reason, the next step | Retry |
| 7 | Findings (medium, low) | as above | Open finding |
| 8 | Start | the PR's title | Start review |
| 8 | Cancelled | the PR's title, "Stopped before it finished. Nothing was posted." | Review again |

A parked review ranks above a critical finding because the reviewer is blocked on the reader, and approving takes seconds. Cards group under five tier labels: Before reviews can run (setup), Decide now, Blocking findings, Unblock, and When you have a minute. Setup is a precondition, not a decision: it has its own tier, the title bar says "Setup needed" when it is all that waits, and no count of decisions includes it. A cancel was the reader's own choice, so a cancelled review waits with the reviews nobody started, in the neutral ink, and starts fresh instead of retrying. The engine records a cancel as a failed run whose reason starts with "cancelled"; `queue::ending` reads it. A tier also sets a card's weight, so the order reads without color: Decide now stands off the column on the strong edge and a two-layer shadow, the middle tiers keep one soft shadow, and When you have a minute lies flat in the column's ink. A running review is never a card. It is a pill in the strip until it needs you. The selected card is filled and wears a 1 pt accent stroke; a selected row anywhere else wears a 3 pt accent rail inside its left edge. A selected card whose page fills the pane docks: its title, quote, reason and button are all on that page, so the card keeps only its kicker and its meta line, and the PR's title appears in the sidebar and the pane only. A parked card docks once its proof is open, and while the approval is on its way it reads "Posting…" and "Approved at f86859b · waiting for GitHub". With no PR selected and setup blocking, the pane shows Settings and the setup card is the selected one. A card's meta line and its action sit on their own rows, so neither cuts the other short.

An empty queue does not get a page of its own. The home shows the done list under a "You're clear" header, so the reader sees what was finished instead of a blank column beside a populated pane.

The same module derives the sidebar rows (`RowState`: running with its phase, parked, failed, cancelled, findings with the worst severity and a count, posted, clean, merged, closed, new), the connection `Stage` (offline, connecting, ready), the gauge's `Mark`s, a finding's `Tracking` state and its evidence count, and the board's sentence. `SetupCount` is the one vocabulary the sidebar badge, the setup card and the setup page share: blocking checks keep reviews from running, limited checks let them run with less ("1 blocking · 2 limited").

## Layout at 1280 × 800

| Region | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Title bar | 52 pt | The traffic lights, then a 20 pt inset on every view, then Back on a deep page (a chevron in a 28 pt square that lights on hover, as a macOS toolbar's Back is, with ⌘[ in its tooltip), the page title (15 pt semibold), its one-sentence state (12 pt, muted), "N running" on a deep page while reviews work, a Read-only chip when the daemon runs no commands (it opens Settings), and the 220 pt ⌘K field. On the home the title says how much waits ("4 decisions waiting", "Setup needed", "You're clear") and nothing under it, since the queue right below says what each decision is. It drags the window. |
| Sidebar | 232 pt | Needs you, Done, Archived, Usage (no count) and Settings with their counts (Archived in the neutral ink) (Settings counts the blocking setup checks), then every PR grouped under its repository's name (11 pt semibold, lowercase as GitHub writes it, the sidebar's one label style): a glyph, the title on one full-width line with the whole title in its tooltip, and under it the number, a state word short enough never to truncate ("To approve", "Collecting", "Cancelled") and the badge or phase pips at the right. Then the account footer. The selected list is the one the pane shows: Needs you over the queue and a PR's timeline, Done over the done list (an empty queue shows it too), Setup while the pane shows setup. |
| Live strip | 44 pt | One pill per running review, two at most, each capped at 520 pt: a breathing dot, the number, the title, the progress gauge, the phase and "step 3 of 6". A third review and on fold under "+N running", which opens the palette's Watch rows. The rule: the strip belongs to the home (the queue with its PR page or timeline). A finding, the proof, the diff and Settings are deep pages, where the strip steps aside and the title bar keeps the one fact, "N running", which opens the first. |
| Queue column | 400 pt | Cards 16 pt apart from a 24 pt top inset, so the first tier label sits on the detail page's first line, a 56 pt bottom inset so the last card ends above the fade, and a fade where more cards scroll under. |
| Detail | the rest | Sections 24 pt apart, prose at a 440 pt measure (68 characters), a 56 pt bottom inset, and the scrollbar shown at rest, so a page that goes on under the bar says so before it scrolls. The action bar is a 56 pt row of the layout, opaque, with a 1 px hairline on top; a 24 pt fade over the end of the scroll area says the page goes on under it. The bar is a list of actions laid out against the window's width: when they don't fit it drops the shortcut hints, then keeps the primary and folds the rest under More ⌄. It never clips. |

A finding opens in two columns: the argument on the left in a 360 pt column (the claim, why the reviewer believes it in the full body color, the suggested fix, the status sentence, the confidence in words, the history), and on the right, about 60 % of the page, the evidence pinned `path:Lstart–end @sha`, the tool summaries, the hunk the finding sits in and Open full diff. The evidence wraps at 71 columns, so the lines the diff shows whole are mostly whole there too. A finding with no evidence says "No code cited" and points at the diff. Every surface counts evidence from one source: the loaded detail's list, and the summary's count until the detail is here.

A finding tracks across the PR's heads. Its chip names the state and the commit the reviewer last looked at it on ("Still present · b131fd0", "Looks resolved · b131fd0", "Uncertain · b131fd0"). A new finding with the fingerprint of a resolved one reads Reopened: its page says on which commit it was verified fixed and on which its code came back, with a button to the resolved finding, which stays under "From an earlier head". Each history row gives the observation's basis in words ("its code was found again", "its cited lines changed", "its fixed code came back") and its commit. Code follows one rule everywhere, in the evidence, the suggested fix, the finding's hunk, a proof comment's context and the diff: a long line soft-wraps at a space, a comma or a bracket (else before an operator), and the continuation starts under the line's own indent plus two with a `↳` in the gutter. Nothing scrolls sideways and nothing clips. Each surface wraps at the columns Menlo 12 fits beside its gutter at 1280 points.

The diff replaces the queue column with a 264 pt file list. Files group under their folder; a row is one 30 pt line with the M/A/D letter, the name (the path is its tooltip), the finding count and the line counts. Lock files, test snapshots and anything under `generated/` or `dist/` fold under one Generated row at the top, since the protocol carries no generated flag and they are rarely read by hand; selecting one opens the group. F, or the header's button, lists only the files with findings; a PR where every file has one is not offered the filter. It draws the cursor's rows with 8 rows of lead, so the anchored line sits in the upper third, and repeats the hunk's `@@` header above them when the lead skipped it. Each row carries one marker: a finding's severity rail and wash, or the cursor's accent rail. The diff's action bar is the same as every other page's: Back, Next finding, Open on GitHub.

The live page's activity track gives every event a 3 pt tick in a slot at least 12 pt wide, the whole slot a hit target; a tick's height and hue name its kind in a legend under the track (phase in the running hue, finding in its severity, run in the accent, tool call short and muted), and while replaying a handle stands on the held tick. The timeline starts every row's words at one x, with a tool's name in the kind column, and a summary too long for its line loses its middle at a space or a slash. Each run's Verifying section opens with a collapsed "Verifier discarded N candidates" row and what the verifier kept, carried, resolved and reopened, from the run's CANDIDATES_DISPOSED. The daemon streams those counts, not each discard's reason, so the open row hands over `pr-review inspect --pr <repo>#<n>`, which lists every discard and why.

The gauge only claims what the run did. It draws one mark per step from the run's status and publication: done, now, waiting, stopped and skipped. A completed review that posted nothing shows Post as a dashed step labelled "Not posted"; a stop colors only the step it stopped in, in red for a failure and in the muted ink for a cancel, with the steps before it muted.

## The account footer

`src/account.rs` closes the sidebar with one 52 pt row under a hairline: who the daemon reads and publishes as, and how the window reaches it.

| Part | Look |
|---|---|
| Face | A 24 pt circle at the left. A github.com account shows its avatar, clipped round. Until the image is on disk, or when it fails to decode, the circle shows the login's first letter, semibold in the accent over the accent's wash with an outline at twice the wash, the chip's recipe, so nothing moves when the image lands. With no account, the circle is an outline in `border_strong` around a person glyph in the muted ink. |
| Name | The login at 13 pt medium in the text color, cut at its end. With no account, the words in the muted ink: "No GitHub account", "Env token" or "No account reported" (no snapshot yet). An account whose state is not ok adds its state word ("Error", "Unknown") beside the login in the warn color. |
| Connection | Under the name at 11 pt: the 7 pt state dot, the link word in the muted ink ("Live", "Offline", "Stopped by you"), a faint dot, the endpoint's kind ("socket"), and "Read-only" in the warn color on a daemon that runs no commands. |

The account is `Model::identity`, the one rule the post sheet and the terminal client's "publishes as" also read: the daemon's env token wins, then the selected account, then the github.com account gh marks active. The whole row is one tab stop with the button role and a label that reads it as a sentence ("Signed in to GitHub as diego-arbio-dev, connection live, read-only"). A click or ⏎ opens Settings, the page with the GitHub accounts. The tooltip gives the account id, its scopes, the full endpoint and "Click for Settings".

## Tokens (`src/tokens.rs`)

- **Color.** Dark is blue-graphite (`bg #0F1217`, `surface #161A21`). Light is warm paper (`bg #F5F4F0`, `surface #FFFFFF`, a warm gray `#E9E7E0` for the selection, so selecting a card never reads as the lavender of a decision). Each hue has one meaning: accent (periwinkle `#8E95FF` dark, indigo `#4247C8` light) means "needs you" and the primary action; cyan means running; green means done or OK; amber means setup or a warning; red means failed, and filled red is the one irreversible button. Severity has its own set: critical rose, high orange, medium gold and low steel. A chip or a count badge is its hue as text over a wash of the same hue (`tint`: 14 % dark, 12 % light) inside an outline at twice that alpha. A control or a palette row that can't act now is set in `disabled`, the text color dimmed to about half and stored as one opaque color, with an outline of the same color at 80 %. Small secondary text (muted and faint, set at 11 and 12 pt) keeps 5.5:1 on every surface: measured on the renders, antialiasing leaves an 11 pt stroke at about 0.85 of its token's ratio, so the strokes the eye reads stay above 4.5:1. A test checks every text token on every surface it sits on, including chips over their wash, disabled text and its outline (3:1), the syntax colors and the gutter on the code background, and the diff's text over its added and deleted washes, at 4.5:1 or more in both appearances. The light added-line wash is a desaturated green, so it tints without shouting.
- **Severity is never only a color.** Each severity has a shape and a word: ◆ Critical, ▲ High, ● Medium, ○ Low. The card's 3 pt left rail, the diff's anchored line wash and the gutter bar all use that severity's hue.
- **Type.** The interface is set in the system sans (SF Pro) at 11, 12, 13 (body), 15 and 18. What is said is set in the system serif (New York) at 24: page and PR titles, finding titles, the quoted finding on a blocking card, the numbers on the board. Code and ids use Menlo 12 on a 19 pt line. Prose sits on a 21 pt line. Every number that sits in a column or beside another number (the live stats, count badges, the replay counter, line numbers) uses tabular figures, so digits line up and nothing shifts as they change.
- **Spacing.** A 4 pt grid: 16 pt between cards, 24 pt between detail sections.
- **Radii.** Two: 8 for controls, chips and rows; 12 for cards, code, sheets and the palette. Dots and count badges are circles.
- **Titles.** A serif title that needs more than one line wraps at a measure that splits its characters evenly, so no word sits alone on the last line.
- **Motion.** The live dot breathes over 1.6 s. A log row that just landed fades in over 150 ms, and wears the running hue on its left edge while it is the newest. A spinner turns while the window retries or connects. Nothing else moves. Reduce Motion, `PR_REVIEW_REDUCE_MOTION=1` or a snapshot stills all three. Entering replay moves nothing: the activity header keeps one height whether it follows or replays.
- **Window.** The window's background is blurred (`WindowBackgroundAppearance::Blurred`), and the sidebar and title bar let it through at 90 % so the desktop tints them faintly; every text token keeps AA against the solid color underneath. gpui-kit's root paints the theme's opaque background over the window, which hid the blur entirely, so a live window clears it. Measured on a live capture over a saturated gradient, the sidebar's muted text holds 6.5:1 in dark and 4.9:1 in light, the repository labels 6.5:1 and 5.1:1. A snapshot's window is opaque, since its pixels must not depend on what is behind it.

## Themes

A theme is data. `tokens::Theme` holds an id (what the config stores), a name (what the picker, the menu and the palette show), whether it is dark, and a function that returns its `Colors`, the semantic tokens every view paints with: the window, sidebar, surfaces and selection, three text tiers, the accent and focus ring, the status hues, the four severities, the diff's washes and the syntax colors. `THEMES` lists Light, Dark, Solarized Light, Solarized Dark, Nord and Dracula. The reader picks a `Choice`: System, which is Light or Dark as macOS is and follows it when it switches, or one theme. `ui::colors` resolves the choice once per frame, and `apply_theme` also paints gpui-kit's own component colors (background, borders, inputs, tooltips, scrollbar thumbs, the kit's focus ring) from the same palette, so a kit component never shows its default look under a classic theme. Nothing outside `tokens.rs` branches on dark or light. Adding a theme is a row in `THEMES` and a palette function; the picker, View → Appearance, the palette rows and the contrast tests pick it up.

Settings → General holds a tile per choice, each a miniature of the theme in its own colors (its window, a card with two lines of text, its accent and the four severity dots), the one in use outlined in the accent with a check beside its name. A click, View → Appearance (whose item for the theme in use carries the check) or the palette's "Theme: Nord" rows apply it at once and write `theme` into the `[desktop]` section of the config file. The write edits that one line and refuses unless the result parses to the old values plus the theme, so the reader's other keys and comments survive. `--appearance` overrides the saved theme, so a snapshot never depends on the machine.

The keyboard focus ring is the macOS accent where it holds 3:1 on the theme's window, else the theme's `focus` token.

**The canonical palettes against the contrast gate.** The contrast test runs on every theme. Each surface pairing it covers must pass: 4.5:1 for text, 5.5:1 for small muted and faint text, and 3:1 for the disabled outline and the focus ring. A second test keeps every pair of severity hues at least 20 apart in CIE76 ΔE. Solarized and Nord were built for code on one background, and most of their accents and their secondary greys fail AA on the surfaces around a selected card. Each failing token was moved in CIE Lab lightness only, in half-point steps, hue and chroma kept, until it passed. Darker in a light theme, lighter in a dark one. A tool did the search; `tokens.rs` marks every moved token "from <canonical name>".

## Interaction

- **Keyboard first.** J and K walk the cards, and ⏎ does what the card's button says. N and ⇧N go to the next and previous finding, D opens the diff at the finding, T the timeline and, from the timeline, back to the findings, ⌘[ goes back (esc too), and S, ⇧R, C and P start, retry, cancel and preview. ⇥ moves between controls. ⌘K and / open the palette. ⌘1, ⌘2 and ⌘3 switch between Needs you, Done and Archived, ⌘4 opens Usage (D, W and M group it by day, week and month, and T, R, A and P show it over time or split by repository, author or PR, where a PR's row opens it), and E archives the open PR or unarchives an archived one, with no sheet, since Unarchive undoes it. ⌘N adds a PR by its URL, ⌘, opens Settings on the tab last used (1 to 4 switch its tabs while no field has the keyboard; ⌘⌥← and → were the other option, but digits are free there and match the tab row's key caps), ⌘⇧O opens the PR on GitHub, ⌘R starts the reviewer when nothing is listening and refreshes otherwise, ⌘W closes the window, ⌘Q quits and ? shows every shortcut. ⌘O stays free because on macOS it means Open a file, and a reader who presses it expecting that should get nothing surprising. The ⌘ shortcuts are the menu bar's items.
- **Three states, three looks.** ⇥ and ⇧⇥ are the window's own bindings in its key context, ahead of the gpui-kit root's. Hover lifts a surface one step: its fill moves 5 % toward the text color, opaque, and its border stays (pressed is an 8 % wash). Keyboard focus is a 2 pt ring 2 pt off the control's edge, in the macOS accent color the reader picked, so focus looks as it does in every other app; an accent that falls under 3:1 against either window background (yellow on paper) gives way to the brand accent, and so does every snapshot, whose pixels must not depend on the machine. GPUI paints a drop shadow under the whole element, so the ring is the border of a frame 4 pt larger than the control and pulled back by as much: it never fills the control and it clears when focus moves. The window owns the keyboard state: ⇥ turns the ring on and a mouse press turns it off. GPUI's own focus-visible also drops on any pointer move, and macOS sends one when the window activates, which hid the ring a frame after ⇥. Selected is the accent stroke on a card or the accent rail on a row, over the selected fill.
- **Mouse complete.** Every action is also a button or a palette row. A click selects a card, and a second click does what its button says. Icon buttons carry tooltips. Rows are 44 pt where the list allows it. A toast is a raised surface with the sheet's outline, anchored in the detail pane 16 pt above its action bar.
- **Friction where it's irreversible.** Posting to GitHub is the one irreversible act, but it destroys nothing, so Post to GitHub is the primary accent in the sheet and on the proof's bar alike. The proof page's Post opens a sheet that drops from under the title bar, as an NSWindow sheet does, its top edge flush there, with a strong outline and a two-layer shadow that holds in dark. It names the account, the commit, the counts of body and inline comments and the review kind, says beside a warning icon that it can't be undone, and follows macOS: Cancel is the default and shows esc (⏎ closes it too), and Post acts only on ⌘⏎, which sends the reducer's own `Confirm`. ⌘⏎ both opens the sheet from the proof and posts from it, so a quick double press, or a held chord's key repeat, would post before the sheet could be read. A sheet that ⌘⏎ opened arms the chord only once ⏎ has come up and 500 ms have passed; one opened by a click or Y needs only the delay. A different confirm chord was the other option, but ⌘⏎ is the one macOS readers expect on a default-destructive sheet, and the release rule costs a deliberate press nothing. While the sheet is open only its controls are tab stops, so ⇥ stays in it. The scrim covers the whole window, title bar included, at 40 % ink in light and 50 % in dark; the page under it keeps its own look, so nothing restyles behind the sheet. While the approval is on its way the proof says posting everywhere (the title bar, the kicker, the title, "is commenting…", the card and the sidebar row) and every control on its bar is off, esc included. Canceling a running review asks the same way: Keep running on esc and ⏎, Cancel review in filled red on ⌘⏎. Starting and retrying are cheap and reversible, so they go straight through: the reducer's confirmation opens and the desktop answers it at once; a quiet toast says it is sending, and the sheet shows only if the daemon refuses. Every reducer guard stays authoritative: the desktop never bypasses `can_mutate`, `can_confirm` or the confirmation flow. GPUI has no backdrop blur for an element, so the scrim darkens without blurring.
- **Adding a PR.** ⌘N, the palette's "Review a PR by its URL…", the sidebar's row under Settings and the empty state's button open one sheet. It drops from the title bar like the post sheet, with a single mono field and the caret where typing lands. The line under the field reads the text as it is typed: "acme/web · #77" under a pull-request glyph once it names a PR, "already here" with the title for one the window tracks (⏎ opens it), and a warning that says what a PR link looks like while it names none. ⌘V pastes, dropping a copied line break. ⏎ sends the reducer's `Msg::AddPr`; while the daemon reads the PR from GitHub a spinner says so and the button reads "Adding…". The sheet closes when the reducer selects the new PR, whose review is starting. A refusal stays in the sheet in the danger hue, a short title for its code ("GitHub won't show this PR to the daemon") over the daemon's own sentence, which names the account it read as, and editing the URL clears it. The sheet shows its outcome in place, so no toast repeats it under the scrim. On a read-only daemon the row, the button and the palette row are locked with the reason, and ⌘N shows the read-only toast.
- **The palette explains itself.** Unavailable actions stay listed in the disabled color, with the reducer's reason ("its latest run has not failed") spaced off the key hint; on a read-only daemon the group's header says it once. Every row has a glyph, so an action, a place to go and a view read apart. The chosen row is the selection fill and rail with its text at full strength, and the pointer moves it, so a hovered row and the chosen row are never two rows. On a read-only daemon every command row carries its own lock and "read-only daemon", with the fix in its tooltip. The list ends on a whole row, under a fade when more scroll; a row without a shortcut leaves the slot blank. The characters the query lit are set bold when they make one run or word prefixes; letters scattered through words stay plain. A query matches a title's prefix first, then word prefixes, then word initials, then a scattered subsequence; groups stay together under their best match.

## States

- **Offline.** No daemon answers and no snapshot ever came: a 640 pt column centered in the pane with a disconnected glyph, "Reviewer offline" with the reason as a sentence, a spinner that says the app retries on its own, then Start reviewer ⌘R as the primary action and Settings. The daemon command waits under "Run it yourself", in a copyable block with its socket shell-quoted. The sidebar's lists dim, since nothing sits behind them; the window never claims the reader is clear. ⌘R is Start here rather than Retry: the window already retries on its own, so the one key that helps when nothing listens is the one that starts something. With a daemon up, ⌘R refreshes as before.
- **Starting, stopping, stopped, start failed.** What the app asked of the daemon reads apart from what the link does. While a start is on its way, the offline column says "Starting the reviewer" under a spinner and names the socket it waits on; the window dials the moment the socket appears and gives up after 20 s. A stop shows "Stopping the reviewer" as a quiet toast. A stop the reader asked for reads "Reviewer stopped" in the muted ink under a stop glyph, never as offline or as a crash, both on the offline column and as the board's banner, with Start reviewer beside it; the sidebar's connection says "Stopped by you". A start that never answered reads "The reviewer didn't start" in red, with the reason and the log's path, and Open the log. The stop sheet follows the cancel sheet: Keep running is the default on esc and ⏎, and the stop is filled red on ⌘⏎. It opens only when the stop costs something: running reviews pause (it says they resume on the next start), or the LaunchAgent's KeepAlive would restart the daemon at once, where the sheet offers Stop and keep stopped instead.
- **Connecting.** Skeletons hold the shapes the snapshot will fill: the strip's pill, the queue's cards, the page's header, tab row, run block and finding rows, at the same radii and borders, so nothing jumps on connect.
- **Clear.** Only with a snapshot: "You're clear · N PRs reviewed" over the done list. With nothing tracked yet it points at ⌘N and carries a Review a PR… button.
- **No findings.** A reviewed PR with nothing to judge says what the reviewer read and verified, under a check; a PR no review has looked at says so under a dashed circle, never a check.
- **Failed.** The gauge colors only the phase the run stopped in, taken from the last phase change the window saw. The run block carries the reason and the next step once; the sentence under the title says only that the review did not finish.
- **Too large.** A review the daemon refused for size reads "Too large to review" with the reason and the next step: exclude generated files or split the PR, then retry.
- **Read-only daemon.** A daemon is read-only when it says so or when it answers live and grants none of start, retry, cancel and post (the fixture daemon). Every command stays visible as a dashed button with a lock and the fix in its tooltip, on the cards and the bars alike, the title bar wears a Read-only chip, and a refused key shows a toast in the desktop's words: "Read-only daemon: starting reviews is off. Restart it with --allow-commands." Review post still opens the proof, since reading it is not a command. The daemon's own wording never reaches the page.
- **Posted.** The proof's kicker turns to PUBLISHED with the URL, the title reads "Posted to acme/shop #1291", one sentence gives the account, the commit and the counts, and the title bar says "posted"; the Done list holds the PR.
- **Settings.** A segmented control under the title (the Usage page's, with a count badge) splits the page into four tabs, each scrolling on its own from its top: Review (Agent, Risk check, Review message; the default), Reviewer (the setup checks, then the daemon with Start, Restart and Stop), Accounts and Appearance. The tab lasts the session and is never saved. Arriving on Settings while reviews can't run (a blocking check, a read-only daemon, a link that is down) opens Reviewer, so the blocking checks are in front; the setup card, the Read-only chip and the account footer open the tab they are about. A tab with something that stops reviews carries a red count, so no such problem hides behind a tab the reader isn't on: Reviewer counts the blocking checks (at least 1 for a read-only or unreachable daemon), Review a missing model or Jev check. A limited check (no Jev key, a public-only token) can be a lasting choice, so it is listed in its tab but never counted: a badge that never clears stops being read. The tabs show no key caps beside the counts; their keys (1–4) are in the tooltips and the ? sheet. The palette has a row per tab; picking a theme or starting the reviewer there acts at once, without opening Settings.
- **Setup checks.** Settings lists them on its Reviewer tab. A check's fix renders every command and environment variable as a mono chip with a copy button, and the prose around them as prose; Copy all commands copies every command the open checks name, one per line.
- **Review message.** Settings → Review has a section under Agent and Risk check for the template of the review body the reviewer posts. The editor colours every `{{name}}` in place, a known one in the accent and an unknown one in the danger hue, through gpui-component's editor text decorations. The variables the daemon lists sit under it, and a click puts `{{name}}` at the caret. A preview fills the template with the daemon's samples, using the daemon's own rule, and a unit test holds it to the daemon's rendering. Save stays off and says why when nothing changed, a variable is unknown, the text is over 4000 characters or the daemon is read-only. Reset to default restores the built-in message. A refusal shows the daemon's sentence. While the editor has the keyboard, the page's single-key shortcuts stay out of it.
- **Automatic reviews.** Settings → Reviewer ends with a card for the daemon's polling: a switch with what it does and what it costs, a line on what posting does with these reviews (muted under approval, the warn hue when they post unasked), the interval as the Usage page's segmented control, the daily cap as a second segmented control, a team-requests switch, and a combobox for each repository list. Everything saves at once, as macOS settings do. A combobox is the list's chips (mono, on the selection fill, each with a 24 pt × named "Remove owner/name" in its tooltip), then a search field, then, while the field has the keyboard, a dropdown that floats over the card from the field, the way a menu does: below the field, or above it when the window has no room below, never over the field, kept inside the window, eight rows tall with its own scroll. It lists the account's repositories (`repos.list`, asked once per connection) that match the typed characters in order, an exact match first and then the most recently pushed first, a lock on private ones, at most 50. ↑ and ↓ move the lit row (the selection fill and the focus ring), skipping rows that can't be picked and stopping at the ends, ⏎ adds the lit row, esc closes the list and a second esc leaves the field. A press on a row or on a chip's × acts without taking the keyboard from the field, so the reader can keep typing; a press anywhere else closes the list. With the field empty, ⌫ removes the last chip and ← puts the keyboard on it: the chip carries the focus ring, ← and → walk the chips, ⌫ and ⌦ remove the one with the ring, and → past the last or esc goes back to the field. A repository already in the other list is set in `disabled` and says "already in Only" or "already in Never". A full `owner/name` that no row names is offered last as "Add owner/name", so ⏎ adds a repository the list misses or couldn't load. Anything else ⏎ can't add is said under the field in the danger hue. Until the list comes the dropdown says "Loading repositories…", and a failed list is one muted sentence, "Couldn't load repositories: why. Type owner/name to add one.", over the free-text path. While a field has the keyboard, the page's single-key shortcuts stay out of it. One save is in flight at a time and each carries both lists whole, so changes made during a save are sent after it and the last one wins. The last poll sits under a hairline: a check, its counts and the day's starts in the text color; a slashed circle in the warn hue and "Daily cap reached (20 of 20 today). Resumes tomorrow."; or the error in the danger hue. The daemon's own sentence for a failed poll reaches the page, since it names the limit or the account.
- **Cancelled.** "Cancelled during Analyze. Nothing was posted." in the muted ink under a slashed circle, with Review again (⇧S), since the reader chose to stop it.

## Trust

- The proof shows the body and every inline comment exactly as GitHub will render them, the marker comment GitHub hides, and the account that posts them, with M for the raw markdown. Its kicker reads "Proof · nothing is posted yet" until it is.
- A finding shows its confidence as a number and a sentence, and its status as a word and the sentence that explains it: still present, looks resolved, reopened, uncertain. The history lists every run that judged it.
- Every sentence the app writes about the reviewer's state comes from the model, never from the desktop's own bookkeeping.
